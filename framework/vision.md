# Product direction for v0.5

Updated 21 October 2026. Direction below records the user's requests; detailed rules remain proposals until reviewed and implemented.

## Purpose and audience

Help UX designers start a project well and facilitate the product team through useful next steps. Research is a major part of that journey. Project launch also needs the crucial context, purpose, users, scope, success, responsibilities and constraints that make the work possible.

A person should get value when starting from nothing, joining an existing project, planning research, interpreting evidence or bringing findings to a decision.

## Two connected experiences

**Project launch:** a broad basket of what to consider and how to proceed. Show the overall path, identify crucial missing information, acknowledge what is already done and make the first useful action clear. Broad coverage does not mean every possible workshop or method is mandatory.

**Research builder:** the strongest opportunity for a tailored working plan. Let the designer select relevant questions, activities or methods, add them to a basket and assemble an editable research plan. Each item brings its purpose, required inputs, expected output, guided activity, guides, templates and contextual tips.

The basket is the shared working selection of intended work. The wording "basket" is a working metaphor; its interface label is still open. A catalogue item, a selected plan item and a completed outcome are different things.

## Intended experience

1. Bring a little context and say what is already in place.
2. See the relevant outcomes, missing essentials and suggested next step.
3. Select useful work into the basket; retain control over scope and choices.
4. Review an ordered plan, dependencies, feasibility and limits.
5. Carry out the work through guided activities and supporting resources.
6. Review outputs against outcomes, address blockers and update the next step.

Guides explain how and why. Templates structure an output. Activities help carry out the work. Outcomes establish what the project needs to have in place. Completing one is not automatic proof of completing the others.

## Principles

- Begin with the user's situation and intended decision. Ask for additional detail when it affects a useful recommendation.
- Make progress checkable and allow existing work to count, with a reference or explicit confirmation.
- Give one clear next action, its reason and any important prerequisites. Keep navigation open.
- Adapt the proposal to access, remaining time, available effort, material, evidence and intended claim. A large menu is available; the selected plan remains focused.
- Explain missing information, limits and escalation when they affect the work. Unknown information first calls for a check; an unresolved blocker calls for a response and owner.
- Keep the guided activity feature central. At closeout, review its output and update the plan.
- Preserve manual edits, selected work, notes and portable project files when suggestions change.

## Boundaries for the next release

The work covers project launch and the UX/research contribution with the product team. It does not yet specify a complete engineering delivery system, enterprise collaboration backend or replacement for an approved evidence repository.

These behaviours are planned for v0.5; the current application remains v0.3.4.
